# Excerpt: Managed services pitch (delivery-anchored voice)

Managed services proposals tend to describe a philosophy. We'd rather describe a Tuesday.

A patch ships from IBM for Maximo 7.6.1.2. Here's what happens on our side, in order: it lands in our test environment (a mirror of your Attachment C configuration, not a generic sandbox) within 48 hours. It gets applied against a snapshot of your actual workflows — the ones your team uses, not a demo dataset. If it's clean, it's scheduled into your next maintenance window and you get a two-line notice: what changed, what to watch for. If it's not clean, you hear about the conflict before it ever reaches production, with our recommended fix already attached.

That's the whole model. No quarterly "health check" theater — the health check is what we do every time something changes.

**On the monthly hour cap:** we don't want you rationing support requests to stay under a number. Severity 1 issues (production down, data at risk) are uncapped, full stop — they don't come out of your monthly allotment, because an SLA that makes you hesitate before reporting an outage isn't a real SLA. The capped hours are for routine administration and minor upgrades, which is where a fixed monthly fee actually belongs.

We'll use your ticketing system, not ours — the point of managed services is fewer new tools for your team, not more.
